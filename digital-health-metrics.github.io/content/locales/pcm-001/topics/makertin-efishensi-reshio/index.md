# Marketing Efficiency Ratio

Marketing efficiency ratio (MER) na total revenue wey dem divide by total marketing spend across every channel, for defined period. E dey exist as deliberate independent reality check on platform-reported return on ad spend (ROAS), wey dey measure each channel own claimed contribution to revenue and wey structurally dey prone to over-credit itself — MER instead dey look at total revenue against total spend, figure wey no single ad platform fit distort.

## Why e matter

Ad platforms dey each report ROAS using dia own attribution model, and because most organizations dey run several channels at di same time, di same converting customer dey frequently get credit from more than one platform, so di sum of each platform self-reported ROAS routinely dey overstate total marketing contribution when dem compare am against actual total revenue. MER dey avoid dis problem entirely by to compare total revenue to total spend for organization level, wey make am far harder to distort through attribution gaming or platform-favourable reporting, and na exactly why finance and executive teams dey increasingly treat am as di trustworthy top-line efficiency check wey dem dey use calibrate individual platform-reported figures. Marketing team wey report strong ROAS on every individual channel while overall MER dey decline get real efficiency or attribution-overlap problem wey channel-level reporting alone no go surface.

## How dem dey calculate am

```
MER = total revenue / total marketing spend (all channels, same period)

Unlike ROAS, MER is not calculated per channel — it is a single,
organization-wide figure precisely because its value comes from being
immune to any one platform's attribution claims.

A rising MER over time, at stable or growing spend, indicates improving
overall marketing efficiency; a stable MER at growing revenue can
indicate that growth is coming from non-marketing-driven sources
(e.g. referrals, organic search, word of mouth).
```

## Worked example

One digital health company generate $2,400,000 revenue inside one quarter, with total marketing spend across all paid channels of $480,000. MER na $2,400,000 / $480,000 = 5.0. Individually, di paid search platform report ROAS of 6.0, di paid social platform report ROAS of 5.5, and programmatic display platform report ROAS of 4.0 — if dem just sum dem as claim on total revenue contribution, dem go imply more total attributed revenue than wetin di company actually generate, because meaningful share of converting customers see more than one channel and dem dey count dem two or three times. Di organization-wide MER of 5.0 na di number wey reconcile dis: e no fit inflate by attribution overlap di way platform-level ROAS figures structurally fit.

## Data sources and caveats

Total revenue dey come from di organization own finance or billing system, and total marketing spend dey come from actual invoiced and paid marketing costs across every channel — dem suppose source both independently of any ad platform own dashboard. Dem suppose track MER over time as trend instead of to judge am against one single universal benchmark, because "good" MER dey vary enormously by business model, margin structure, and growth stage: early-stage company wey dey invest heavily for growth fit deliberately accept lower MER than mature company wey dey optimize for profitability. MER no dey diagnose which channel dey responsible for change for efficiency — dat diagnostic work still require channel-level analysis, ideally supplemented by incrementality testing (holdout groups wey no receive any marketing exposure) instead of platform-reported attribution alone.

## Pitfalls

- **To treat platform-reported ROAS as additive across channels**: to sum each platform self-reported ROAS dey overstate total marketing contribution whenever customer see and platform credit am from more than one channel, wey common; MER dey avoid dis by design.
- **To compare MER against fixed universal benchmark**: appropriate MER dey vary by business model, margin, and growth stage; use am as trend for one organization over time instead of fixed pass/fail threshold.
- **To use MER as channel-level diagnostic**: MER na deliberately organization-wide figure and e no fit by itself identify which channel dey drive efficiency change; pair am with channel-level analysis and incrementality testing for dat purpose.
- **To ignore non-marketing revenue drivers**: stable or improving MER at growing revenue fit reflect organic growth (referrals, word of mouth, earned media) instead of marketing efficiency; disaggregate revenue by acquisition source where possible to avoid to over-credit marketing.
