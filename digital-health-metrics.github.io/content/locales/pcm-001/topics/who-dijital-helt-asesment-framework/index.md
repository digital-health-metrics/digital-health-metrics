# WHO Digital Health Assessment Framework

World Health Organization (WHO) dey publish body of guidance to assess digital health interventions before, during, and after national-scale deployment, wey cover monitoring-and-evaluation methodology, standard classification of intervention types, and toolkits to judge whether digital health programme don ready to scale — with particular and consistent emphasis on equity, feasibility, and safety, wey reflect WHO mandate to protect population health across countries wey get very different digital and health-system infrastructure. Unlike single named checklist, e better to understand am as coherent family of WHO publications wey organizations wey dey assess national or regional digital health deployment typically dey draw on together.

## Why e matter

Digital health intervention wey perform well for well-resourced pilot setting fit fail, or actively cause harm, when dem deploy am nationally across populations wey get markedly different levels of connectivity, digital literacy, language, and health system capacity — wey be exactly di gap wey WHO guidance dey exist to close, because WHO core mandate na population health equity across very unevenly resourced member states, no be di interests of any single health system or vendor. WHO framing also dey treat safety and feasibility as gating considerations, no be afterthoughts wey dem go assess once intervention don already work: digital health tool must show say e no go worsen inequity or introduce new safety risk before dem consider scale, no be just to show say e effective in aggregate. For any organization wey dey plan national or multi-country digital health deployment, to align evaluation to WHO guidance often also be practical necessity, because na di framework wey ministries of health and multilateral funders most likely to reference or require.

## How dem dey apply am

```
WHO guidance is applied across several linked evaluation activities,
not one single scored checklist:

Classification    — categorizing the intervention against WHO's
                     Classification of Digital Health Interventions,
                     to ensure like-for-like comparison with similar
                     interventions elsewhere

Monitoring and
evaluation design — following WHO's monitoring and evaluation guidance
                     for digital health interventions, covering study
                     design, outcome selection, and data quality

Scale-readiness
assessment        — assessing groundwork, partnerships, financial
                     sustainability, technical architecture, and
                     operational capacity against WHO/ITU-aligned
                     scale-up assessment toolkits

Equity and safety
review            — an explicit, gating assessment of whether the
                     intervention could worsen access or outcome
                     inequities, or introduce clinical or data-privacy
                     safety risk, before scale-up proceeds
```

## Worked example

One ministry of health dey plan to scale maternal health SMS reminder service from single-region pilot to national deployment. To apply WHO classification guidance, dem categorize di intervention clearly as client-facing, targeted-communication digital health intervention, wey allow comparison against similar interventions wey dem evaluate for other places instead of to assess am as if e completely new. Dem build monitoring and evaluation plan wey follow WHO guidance to track both reach and clinical outcome, no be outcome alone. Scale-readiness assessment identify say di pilot region cellular network reliability dey significantly above di national average, wey mean say di equity and safety review flag material risk: to scale nationally without first to assess rural network coverage fit concentrate benefit for regions wey already better-connected and wealthier, wey go widen instead of close existing maternal health equity gap. Dis finding — wey dem surface specifically because dem treat equity as gating criterion instead of afterthought — make di ministry phase di rollout by network-coverage tier instead of to scale uniformly.

## Data sources and caveats

WHO guidance documents dey publicly published and freely available, but to apply dem require local data wey WHO itself no dey supply — connectivity and digital literacy data specific to di deployment country or region, local health system capacity data, and local outcome data — wey mean say di framework dey provide di structure for assessment, no be di data itself. Because WHO guidance dey deliberately general enough to apply across very different national contexts, to apply am well require genuine local expertise and data to avoid generic, checkbox-style assessment wey go miss country-specific risk; WHO-aligned assessment wey dem carry out without local health system and equity data unlikely to surface di risks wey e dem design to catch.

## Pitfalls

- **To treat classification alone as evaluation**: to correctly classify intervention type na starting point for comparison, no be assessment of di intervention safety, effectiveness, or readiness to scale by itself.
- **To treat equity assessment as final add-on instead of gate**: WHO framing dey treat equity risk as reason to slow down or redesign rollout, no be footnote to report after scale-up don already happen; to assess am only retrospectively defeat im purpose.
- **To apply di framework without local context data**: WHO guidance dey intentionally general; without local connectivity, literacy, and health-system-capacity data, assessment against am go become generic checklist exercise instead of genuine risk assessment.
- **To assume say WHO alignment dey substitute for national regulatory or ethics review**: WHO guidance dey inform and structure assessment but e no replace country own required regulatory, data-protection, or ethics approval processes.
