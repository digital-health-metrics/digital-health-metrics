# WHO Digital Health Assessment Framewirk

The World Health Organization (WHO) publishes a body o guidance for assessin digital health interventions afore, durin, an efter national-scale deployment, coverin monitorin-an-evaluation methodology, a standard classification o intervention types, an toolkits for judgin whether a digital health programme is ready tae scale — wi pairticular an consistent emphasis on equity, feasibility, an safety, reflectin WHO's mandate tae protect population health athort kintras wi verra different digital an health-system infrastructure. Unlike a single named checklist, this is better unnerstuid as a coherent faimily o WHO publications that organizations assessin a national or regional digital health deployment typically draw on thegither.

## Why it maitters

A digital health intervention that performs weel in a weel-resourced pilot setting can fail, or actively cause hairm, when deployed nationally athort populations wi merkitly different levels o connectivity, digital literacy, language, an health system capacity — which is exactly the gap WHO's guidance is there tae close, sin WHO's core mandate is population health equity athort verra unevenly resourced member states, no the interests o ony single health system or vendor. WHO's framin an aa treats safety an feasibility as gatin considerations, no afterthochts tae be assessit ance an intervention aareadies warks: a digital health tool maun be shawn no tae mak inequity waur or bring in new safety risk afore scale is considert, no juist shawn tae be effective in aggregate. For ony organization plannin a national or multi-kintra digital health deployment, alignin evaluation tae WHO's guidance is aften an aa a practical necessity, sin it's the framewirk maist likely tae be referred tae or requirit bi ministries o health an multilateral funders.

## Hoo it's applied

```
WHO guidance is applied across several linked evaluation activities,
not one single scored checklist:

Classification    — categorizing the intervention against WHO's
                     Classification of Digital Health Interventions,
                     to ensure like-for-like comparison with similar
                     interventions elsewhere

Monitoring and
evaluation design — following WHO's monitoring and evaluation guidance
                     for digital health interventions, covering study
                     design, outcome selection, and data quality

Scale-readiness
assessment        — assessing groundwork, partnerships, financial
                     sustainability, technical architecture, and
                     operational capacity against WHO/ITU-aligned
                     scale-up assessment toolkits

Equity and safety
review            — an explicit, gating assessment of whether the
                     intervention could worsen access or outcome
                     inequities, or introduce clinical or data-privacy
                     safety risk, before scale-up proceeds
```

## Warked example

A ministry o health plans tae scale a maternal health SMS reminder service frae a single-region pilot tae national deployment. Applyin WHO's classification guidance, the intervention is categorisit clearly as a client-facin, targetit-communication digital health intervention, allowin comparison again similar interventions evaluatit elsewhaur raither than bein assessit as if it wur entirely novel. A monitorin an evaluation plan is biggit follaein WHO guidance tae track baith reach an clinical outcome, no outcome alane. A scale-readiness assessment finds that the pilot region's cellular network reliability is muckle abune the national average, meanin the equity an safety review flags a material risk: scalin nationally wioot first assessin rural network coverage could concentrate benefit in aareadies better-connectit, wealthier regions, wideninin raither than closin an existin maternal health equity gap. This finding — brocht tae licht specifically because equity wis treatit as a gatin criterion raither than an afterthocht — leads the ministry tae phase the rollout bi network-coverage tier raither than scalin uniformly.

## Data sources an caveats

WHO's guidance documents are publicly publisht an freely available, but applyin them needs local data that WHO itsel disnae supply — connectivity an digital literacy data specific tae the deployment kintra or region, local health system capacity data, an local outcome data — meanin the framewirk gies the structure for assessment, no the data itsel. Because WHO's guidance is deliberately general enough tae apply athort verra different national contexts, applyin it weel needs genuine local expertise an data tae avoid a generic, checkbox-style assessment that misses kintra-specific risk; a WHO-alignit assessment cairriet oot wioot local health system an equity data is unlikely tae bring tae licht the risks it's actually designit tae catch.

## Pitfalls

- **Takkin classification alane as evaluation**: correctly classifyin an intervention type is a stairtin pynt for comparison, no itsel an assessment o the intervention's safety, effectiveness, or readiness tae scale.
- **Takkin equity assessment as a final add-on raither than a gate**: WHO's framin treats equity risk as a reason tae slaw doun or redesign a rollout, no a fitnote tae report efter scale-up haes aareadies happened; assessin it only retrospectively defeats its purpose.
- **Applyin the framewirk wioot local context data**: WHO's guidance is intentionally general; wioot local connectivity, literacy, an health-system-capacity data, an assessment again it turns intae a generic checklist exercise raither than a genuine risk assessment.
- **Assumin WHO alignment is a substitute for a national regulatory or ethics review**: WHO guidance informs an structures an assessment but disnae replace a kintra's ain requirit regulatory, data-protection, or ethics approval processes.
