# Return on Investment (ROI) an Value on Investment (VOI)

Return on investment (ROI) is the net financial return a digital health platform generates relative tae its total cost — licensin, hardware deployment, implementation, an ongaein staffin — expressit as a ratio or percentage. Value on investment (VOI) is a braider companion measure that captures returns a pure financial ROI caulculation canna: quality-o-care improvements, patient an clinician satisfaction, health equity impact, an organizational reputation, nane o which convert cleanly intae a dollar figure but aa o which maitter tae whether a digital health investment shuid be considert a success. Thegither they answer twa different, complementary questions: ROI asks "did this pey for itsel financially?"; VOI asks "wis this worth daein, accountin for ilka thing that maitters?"

## Why it maitters

A digital health investment that shaws a puir or negative ROI in its first year isnae automatically a failure, an ane that shaws a strang ROI isnae automatically a success, if the ROI caulculation ignores material value the programme generates ootside pure cost offset — this is precisely the gap VOI is there tae close, an organizations that relie on ROI alane risk defundin programmes wi genuine value that juist haenae yet, or niver will, shaw up as direct financial return. At the same time, VOI is mair subjective an herder tae benchmark than ROI, which maks it vulnerable tae bein uised tae justify continued investment in a programme that isnae actually deliverin measurable value o ony kind; the discipline o reportin baith thegither, wi VOI's components made as concrete an measurable as possible, is whit keeps this framewirk honest raither than becomin a wey tae dodge financial accountability. Lang-term digital health investment decisions — especially in value-based care, whaur quality an equity outcomes are straucht tied tae payment — mair an mair require baith figures side bi side.

## Hoo it's caulculatit

```
ROI = (financial benefit − total investment cost) / total investment cost × 100

Financial benefit sources: cost per episode of care reduction, bed-day
reduction (converted to a cost figure), avoided ED visits (see ED
diversion rate), reduced readmission penalties, and any direct revenue.

Total investment cost: platform licensing, hardware/device deployment
and maintenance, implementation and integration cost, ongoing clinical
and technical staffing.

VOI is not a single formula, but a structured, multi-dimensional
scorecard combining ROI with defined, tracked non-financial value
domains, for example:
  Clinical quality  (e.g. biometric improvement rate, readmission rate)
  Patient experience (e.g. patient net promoter score, SUS score)
  Equity           (e.g. digital access rate, digital literacy rate)
  Workforce         (e.g. physician burnout rate)
Each domain is reported on its own terms, not artificially converted
to a dollar figure, alongside the financial ROI figure.
```

## Warked example

A remote monitorin programme costs $600,000 in its first year (platform licensin, device deployment, an extra clinical staffin) an generates $750,000 in quantifiable financial benefit (bed-day cuttin an avoidit readmission penalties). ROI is ($750,000 − $600,000) / $600,000 × 100 = 25%. Alangside this figure, the organization's VOI scorecard reports a 5-pynt improvement in patient net promoter score, a biometric stabilization rate o 68% for enrolled hypertension patients, an a measured reduction in enrolled clinicians' efter-hours documentation time. Nane o thae three extra findings chynges the 25% ROI figure, but thegither they build a muckle stronger case for continued investment than the ROI figure alane wid support, especially tae stakeholders (clinical leadership, patient advocacy groups) wha weicht quality an equity outcomes as heichly as financial return.

## Data sources an caveats

The financial benefit side o ROI draws on the same cost an outcome data uised elsewhaur in this beuk — cost per episode o care, bed-day cuttin, ED diversion rate — convertit intae a dollar figure uisin the organization's ain cost-accountin methodology, an shuid uise conservative, defensible assumptions raither than optimistic best-case anes, sin an ROI figure biggit on aggressive assumptions winna survive scrutiny frae a sceptical finance team. VOI's non-financial domains shuid ilka uise an aareadies-validatit metric frae elsewhaur in this beuk (biometric stabilization rate, patient net promoter score, digital access rate, physician burnout rate) raither than an informally inventit substitute, sae that the VOI scorecard itsel bides auditable an comparable ower time raither than turnin intae a loosely defined narrative.

## Pitfalls

- **Reportin ROI alane for a programme wi material non-financial value**: this risks defundin a programme wi genuine clinical, equity, or warkforce value juist because that value disnae shaw up in a pure financial caulculation; pair ROI wi a structurit VOI scorecard.
- **Takkin VOI as a wey tae avoid financial accountability**: VOI shuid uise concrete, previously validatit metrics for ilka non-financial domain, no vague or unmeasured claims o value, or it turns intae a wey tae justify continued spendin wioot genuine evidence.
- **Uisin optimistic or unvalidatit assumptions in the ROI cost-avoidance caulculation**: an ROI figure biggit on aggressive assumptions aboot avoidit cost winna staun up tae scrutiny an can dae hairm tae the credibility o future investment cases.
- **Comparin ROI figures athort programmes wi gey different cost structures wioot adjustment**: a programme wi high upfront capital cost an a lang payback period shuidnae be compared on a single-year ROI figure again ane wi low upfront cost an fast payback wioot accountin for the different time horizons involved.
