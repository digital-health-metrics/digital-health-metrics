# RE-AIM Framework

RE-AIM na public health evaluation framework wey dey assess digital health intervention across five distinct dimensions — Reach, Effectiveness, Adoption, Implementation, and Maintenance — instead of through any single metric. E dey exist to correct one specific, common failure mode for digital health evaluation: programme wey report impressive effectiveness data from small, motivated pilot population but wey nobody ever adopt at scale, or wey nobody sustain after im funded pilot period, never actually demonstrate public health value, no matter how good im headline outcome numbers look alone.

## Why e matter

Dem dey frequently evaluate digital health pilots on effectiveness alone — di outcome measure improve for di people wey use am — while dem leave di other four dimensions unmeasured or unreported, wey dey systematically bias di evidence base toward interventions wey work well for ideal, small-scale conditions but wey no tell anything about whether dem work at scale, reach di population wey need dem most, or survive beyond dia initial funding and enthusiasm. Dem develop RE-AIM specifically to force evaluators to report on external validity (e go work for another place, for who) together with internal validity (e work here), wey matter enormously for anybody wey dey decide whether to fund, commission, or scale digital health intervention based on single study or pilot results. Because dem fit measure each of di five dimensions for both individual and organizational or setting level, RE-AIM also dey surface trade-offs — for example, highly effective intervention wey highly selected, highly trained staff deliver (strong effectiveness, weak adoption representativeness) — wey single combined metric go hide entirely.

## How dem dey apply am

```
Reach          = proportion of the eligible target population who
                  participate, and how representative participants are
                  of that eligible population (not just raw
                  participant count)

Effectiveness  = impact on primary outcomes, including quality-of-life
                  and unintended negative consequences, not only the
                  single headline outcome measure

Adoption       = proportion and representativeness of settings and
                  staff/delivery agents who adopt the intervention,
                  relative to all eligible settings and staff

Implementation = consistency and fidelity of delivery as intended,
                  time and cost required to deliver it, and adaptations
                  made along the way

Maintenance    = extent to which the intervention becomes part of
                  routine practice long-term, at both the individual
                  (sustained individual-level effects) and
                  setting/organizational (institutionalization) level
```

## Worked example

Dem pilot one digital diabetes prevention programme across 20 primary care clinics for one region wey get 200 eligible clinics for total. Dem assess Reach as 1,200 out of estimated 15,000 eligible patients across di 20 pilot clinics (8%), and dem find say participants somewhat younger and more digitally literate than di full eligible population — representativeness gap wey worth to note instead of to ignore. Effectiveness show clinically meaningful reduction for mean HbA1c among completers, but high dropout rate mean say dis effect dey diluted when dem assess am on intention-to-treat basis across all enrolled patients, no be just completers. Adoption na 20 out of 200 eligible clinics (10%), wey concentrate for clinics wey get above-average existing digital infrastructure — again, representativeness concern. Implementation fidelity assessment find say most clinics deliver di programme largely as dem design am, though two clinics substantially shorten di onboarding process under time pressure. Maintenance assessment one year after pilot funding end find only 6 of di original 20 clinics still dey actively run di programme — result wey, when dem report am together with di initially promising effectiveness figure, give much more complete and honest picture of di programme real-world prospects than effectiveness data alone for give.

## Data sources and caveats

Each RE-AIM dimension dey typically draw on different data source: Reach and Adoption from enrolment and eligibility records, Effectiveness from clinical or outcome data (often di same data wey dem don already use to calculate specific metric somewhere else for dis book, like biometric improvement rate), Implementation from fidelity checklists, delivery logs, or direct observation, and Maintenance from follow-up assessment wey dem conduct well after di initial evaluation period — wey mean say dem no fit complete full RE-AIM assessment at single point in time and e require make dem plan di evaluation from di start, no be to add am retrospectively. Because to report all five dimensions honestly (including weak results for some) na more work and less flattering than to report only effectiveness, published digital health evaluations dey disproportionately under-report Reach, Adoption, and Maintenance compared with Effectiveness — known publication-bias pattern wey anybody wey dey use RE-AIM-labelled evidence to inform decision suppose watch for.

## Pitfalls

- **To report effectiveness only and call am RE-AIM evaluation**: to use di RE-AIM name while e dey report only one of im five dimensions defeat di framework purpose and dem no suppose describe am as RE-AIM assessment.
- **To measure Reach and Adoption as raw counts instead of proportions of di eligible population**: large raw participant count fit still represent small, unrepresentative fraction of who di intervention suppose ideally reach; always calculate against di full eligible denominator.
- **To assess Maintenance too early**: institutionalization and sustained effect fit only dem judge well after initial funding or novelty effects fade; assessment wey dem take immediately after pilot end no fit talk about Maintenance no matter how di other four dimensions look.
- **To treat di five dimensions as equally weighted or combinable into one score**: RE-AIM na deliberately multi-dimensional profile, no be single number; to combine di dimensions into one composite score dey throw away exactly di trade-off visibility wey di framework dem design to preserve.
