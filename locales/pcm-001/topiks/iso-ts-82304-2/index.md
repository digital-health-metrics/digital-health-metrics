# ISO/TS 82304-2

ISO/TS 82304-2 na international technical specification, wey dem publish under ISO Technical Committee 215 (Health Informatics), wey define structured method to assess di quality of health and wellness applications — wey cover usability, technical robustness and reliability, interoperability, content quality, and data security and privacy — for products wey dey outside di scope of full medical device regulation but still materially affect user health decisions or behaviour. E dey exist to fill one specific gap: di large majority of consumer-facing health and wellness apps (fitness trackers, symptom diaries, wellness coaching apps) no dey regulated as medical devices, yet before no common, structured way dey to assess or compare dia basic quality and safety.

## Why e matter

App stores dey host hundreds of thousands of health and wellness apps wey get enormously variable quality, and before common technical specification exist, patient, clinician, or health system no get structured, comparable way to judge one app basic quality and safety against another beyond star ratings and marketing claims — gap wey matter because poorly designed health app fit still cause real harm (inaccurate content, poor data security, misleading claims) even without to meet di regulatory threshold of medical device. Dem deliberately structure ISO/TS 82304-2 around domains wey non-specialist reviewer fit assess consistently, wey don make am di technical basis for several national and commercial health app quality-labelling and curation services, wey give health systems and app libraries defensible, standardized way to include or exclude apps from recommended list instead of to rely on ad hoc judgement.

## How dem dey apply am

```
Assessment is organized around defined quality domains, evaluated
by structured review rather than a single numeric formula:

Usability                        — clarity, accessibility, and ease
                                    of use for the intended user group
Technical robustness/reliability — stability, performance, and
                                    freedom from technical defects
Interoperability                 — ability to exchange data with other
                                    systems where relevant to the app's
                                    function
Content quality and safety       — accuracy, currency, and absence of
                                    harmful or misleading health claims
Security and privacy             — data protection practice and
                                    transparency about data use

Each domain is scored via structured review criteria and combined
into an overall quality assessment, which several health app
quality-labelling schemes use as the technical basis for a public
quality label or curated-library inclusion decision.
```

## Worked example

One health system digital app library programme want curate recommended list of wellness apps for patients instead of to leave app selection entirely to app-store search. Dem assess each candidate app against di ISO/TS 82304-2 domains: sleep-tracking app score well for usability and technical robustness, adequately for content quality, but dem flag am during di security and privacy review for to share user data with third-party advertisers without clear disclosure — finding wey serious enough to exclude di app from di recommended list despite im otherwise strong usability score. Dis domain-by-domain outcome more actionable for both di curation team and, if dem share am, di app own developer than single blended quality score go be, because e identify precisely which aspect need remediation before dem fit reconsider di app.

## Data sources and caveats

Trained reviewer or accredited assessment service dey typically carry out assessment against ISO/TS 82304-2, following di specification structured review criteria for each domain, and several national and commercial initiatives (health app quality-labelling and curation organizations, some of dem dey operate under formal national health system endorsement) dey use di standard as di technical basis for dia own public-facing app quality labels — wey mean say app "certified" or "labelled" status for practice often dey reflect one specific labelling scheme own implementation of di standard, no be necessarily identical process across every scheme, so dem suppose check and disclose di specific assessing organization and im methodology together with any quality label wey dem cite. Di specification dey assess quality and basic safety characteristics of app as software; e no be substitute for medical device regulatory clearance where app claims or functions actually meet medical device threshold, and to use am as one go be category error.

## Pitfalls

- **To treat quality label as regulatory clearance**: app wey dem assess and label under ISO/TS 82304-2 no thereby receive medical device regulatory approval; di two serve different purposes and dem no suppose ever mix dem for how dem describe or market app.
- **To assume say all labelling schemes wey base on di standard equivalent**: different organizations dey implement ISO/TS 82304-2-based assessment with dia own specific review processes and rigour; check which organization perform assessment and how, instead of to treat any "ISO/TS 82304-2-based" label as interchangeable with any other.
- **To assess only usability while dem neglect security and privacy**: usability issues na di most visible to end user and di easiest to assess informally, wey fit make reviewers under-weight di less visible but potentially more consequential security and privacy domain.
- **To treat di assessment as one-time, permanent certification**: app content, security practices, and third-party data-sharing arrangements fit all change after initial assessment; credible quality-labelling programme dey re-assess periodically instead of to treat initial pass as permanent.
