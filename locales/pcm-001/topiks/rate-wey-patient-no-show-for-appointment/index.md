# Rate wey Patient No Show for Appointment

Appointment no-show rate (dem dey also call am "did not attend", or DNA, rate) na di share of di appointments wey dem don schedule, wey di patient neither come nor cancel with reasonable notice. E be one of di oldest operational metrics for healthcare, and digital tools, especially reminders, self-service rescheduling, and portal-based booking, na dem dey among di most effective and di best-evidenced ways wey dem fit take reduce am now.

## Why e matter

Every no-show na one unit of clinical capacity wey dem no fit usually recover, because most services no fit fill same-day gap for short notice, so di rate dey directly drive how long waiting list go be, di cost per completed appointment, and di clinician time wey dem lose. No-show behaviour no dey spread equally: e dey connect with deprivation, how person fit get transport, caring responsibilities, and di burden of managing plenty long-term conditions. So if dem treat high rate as only patient behaviour problem, instead of partly as signal about access barriers, e dey usually produce interventions (like blanket penalties) wey go make inequity strong pass to reduce am. Digital reminders and easy digital rescheduling dey consistently among di most effective, low-cost interventions wey dey available, and na why dis metric belong inside digital health measurement programme, no be only for operational reporting.

## How dem dey calculate am

```
No-show rate = appointments wey dem mark "did not attend" / total scheduled appointments × 100
```

Dem dey usually remove scheduled appointment from di denominator, or dem go move am go separate category, if either party cancel am with more than defined notice period (commonly 24 hours). Dem dey usually report late cancellations (wey dey below dat notice period) separate from true no-shows, because di operational and behavioural implications no be di same.

## Worked example

One community clinic schedule 2,000 appointments for one month. Out of dem, 140 dem cancel with more than 24 hours' notice (dem rebook dem and remove dem from di denominator), 60 dem cancel late (under 24 hours), and 180 dem record as true no-show wey no contact at all. Di no-show rate na 180 / 2,000 × 100 = 9%. If dem put di 60 late cancellations inside di same category with true no-shows, di rate wey dem report go rise to 12%, and na why dem must always state di definition wey dem use together with di figure.

## Data sources and caveats

Di scheduling or practice management system na di primary source, using im appointment status codes; di quality of di metric depend completely on whether staff dey consistently use di correct status instead of one generic "cancelled" bucket for everything. Organizations wey introduce digital reminders (SMS, app push notification, or portal alerts) suppose measure di no-show rate before and after di change for comparable patient and service mix, because reminder effectiveness dey well documented for randomized and observational studies but e dey different by population and channel.

## Pitfalls

- **To compare raw rates across clinics wey get different overbooking practices**: clinic wey deliberately overbook to cover for expected no-show rate go show different apparent rate from one wey no do so, no matter di true patient behaviour.
- **To mix late cancellations with true no-shows**: di two get different causes and different digital fixes (late-cancellation problem dey often solve with easier self-service rescheduling; true no-show problem dey often solve with better reminders and correct contact details).
- **Survivorship bias from discharge policies**: services wey dey discharge patients after repeated no-shows go see dia own rate improve mechanically, while di same patients just dey move go another place inside di system.
- **To blame di patient for digital exclusion**: patient wey no get smartphone or reliable text service no go benefit from digital-only reminder strategy, so dem usually need multi-channel approach (letter, call, text, app) to avoid make access gaps wide pass.
